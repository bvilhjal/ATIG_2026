def read_gwas(path):
    """Read a GWAS file. Harmonised GWAS Catalog files (*.h.tsv.gz) have GRCh38
    positions, and our LD reference is GRCh37, so for those we match by rsID only."""
    sumstats = allelign.read_sumstats(path)
    if path.name.endswith('.h.tsv.gz'):
        sumstats.chrom[:] = ''
        sumstats.pos[:] = 0
    return sumstats

snps = {}
for trait, h in aligned.items():
    rows = h.var_index  # Rows of the LD reference.
    z = h.beta / h.se
    snps[trait] = pd.DataFrame({'snp': ref.variants.id[rows],
                                'chrom': ref.variants.chrom[rows].astype(int),
                                'pos': ref.variants.pos[rows],
                                'a1': ref.variants.a1[rows],
                                'z': z,

                                'p': 2 * stats.norm.sf(np.abs(z)),

def manhattan(ax, table):
    """Plot the 1% of SNPs with the smallest P values along the genome."""
    top = table.nsmallest(len(table) // 100, 'p')
    x = top['chrom'] + top['pos'] / 250e6        # Chromosomes side by side.
    y = -np.log10(top['p'].clip(lower=1e-50))     # P below 1e-50 is drawn at 50.
    colour = np.where(top['chrom'] % 2 == 0, 'grey', 'steelblue')
    ax.scatter(x, y, c=colour, s=4)
    ax.axhline(-np.log10(5e-8), color='red')      # Genome-wide significance.
    ax.set_xticks(range(1, 23))
    ax.set_ylabel('-log10 P')


fig, axes = plt.subplots(3, 1, figsize=(10, 7), sharex=True)

Q4. Heritability¶

LDSC regresses each SNP's χ² on its LD score $\ell_j$:

$$E[\chi^2_j] = 1 + Na + \frac{N h^2}{M}\,\ell_j,$$

where $N$ is the sample size and $M$ the number of SNPs. The slope gives the heritability $h^2$, and the intercept $1 + Na$ measures confounding (Table 4, Figure 2).

Which trait is most heritable? Is there much confounding? Set M = ref.m_snps and rerun: what changes?

    fit = fits[trait]

Q5. Genetic correlations¶

Bivariate LDSC regresses the product of two traits' z scores on LD score. The slope, scaled by the two heritabilities, gives the genetic correlation $r_g$, and the intercept reflects people who are in both GWAS (Table 5, Figure 3).

Which traits are genetically correlated? UK Biobank is in both the BMI and the DBP GWAS, and DBP was adjusted for BMI. Can you explain the BMI–DBP result?

rg_table = {}
for trait1, trait2 in [('BMI', 'TG'), ('BMI', 'DBP'), ('TG', 'DBP')]:  # About a minute.
    fit = ldsc_rg_sumstats(gwas[trait1], gwas[trait2], ref.variants, ref.scores,

Data and methods¶

All three GWAS are European-ancestry meta-analyses from the GWAS Catalog (GCST006900, GCST002216, GCST006630; see data/sources.json), downloaded with allelign. The LD scores are the 1000 Genomes European scores released with LDSC (Zenodo 7768714); PLDSC leaves out about 30,000 SNPs in long-range LD regions such as the MHC, and SNPs with χ² above max(80, N/1000). Background: Bulik-Sullivan et al. 2015a and 2015b.
